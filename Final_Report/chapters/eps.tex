\chapter{Electrical Power System (EPS)}

\section{Design Overview}

The EPS delivers a single regulated 5~V rail to the platform from a
two-cell parallel Li-ion pack, with fault protection and battery-voltage
telemetry to the OBC (IF-008). It is sized against MR-002 (six-hour flight),
PR-008 ($\geq 1.5\times$ power margin), IF-009 (5~V rail behaviour under
load), SR-001/002/003 (over-current, reverse-polarity, over-discharge
protection), and CR-001 (mass).

The pack is 2$\times$ Panasonic NCR18650B (unprotected, parallel,
2$\times$3400~mAh, 25.1~Wh nominal), chosen for energy density with
well-characterised cold performance ($-20$\textdegree{}C rated, so the
battery is not the platform's thermal bottleneck) and a hermetically sealed
can that tolerates the $\sim$1~kPa stratospheric environment without LiPo's
pouch-swell risk. A USB power bank, LiPo pouch, and lithium-primary cells
were all rejected on cold performance, altitude-pressure risk, and
non-rechargeability respectively. Because the cells are unprotected,
over-current, reverse-polarity, and over-discharge protection is entirely
external (Section~\ref{sec:eps-architecture}). A boost-only (not
buck-boost) topology is used because the battery voltage (3.0--4.2~V) is
always below the 5~V rail target.

\section{Power Budget}

The initial budget overestimated the payload: it modelled the Edge TPU at
full inference power (1.8~W) for 40\% of the flight. A review corrected
this to the datasheet active-power figure ($\sim$3~W) at a realistic
duty -- Edge TPU inference latency is millisecond-scale against a 1~Hz
capture cycle, so the TPU is only briefly active per second -- and removed
a duplicated boost-efficiency term from the battery-referred conversion.

The corrected budget, referred to the battery: Coral $\sim$0.76~W (measured
158--180~mA idle; inference adds $\sim$0.3~A for 2--2.4~ms, negligible in
the 1~Hz average), Nucleo + sensors + LoRa $\sim$0.40~W, regulator quiescent
and series losses $\sim$0.03~W -- \textbf{$\sim$1.2~W total}. Over MR-002's
six-hour flight this is $\sim$7.3~Wh against $\sim$22.6~Wh usable, a
$\sim$3.1$\times$ margin against the PR-008 floor of 1.5$\times$.

\section{Power Architecture}
\label{sec:eps-architecture}

The battery's 2P pack feeds a 4~A polyfuse (FUZETEC FRG40016F), an SI2301
P-MOSFET reverse-polarity stage, and a Pololu U3V40F5 5~V boost regulator.
The regulator output feeds the Coral payload (gated by a GPIO-switched
high-side P-MOSFET) and the Nucleo's E5V input; the Nucleo derives 3.3~V
on-board for the sensors, SD card, and LoRa module. A 22~k$\Omega$-top
voltage divider taps the post-fuse battery line to an OBC ADC input
(IF-008) at a firmware multiplier of 3.2.

\textbf{Protection} is layered: the 4~A polyfuse handles over-current
(SR-001); the SI2301 stage is the sole reverse-polarity protection
(SR-002), the regulator itself provides none; over-discharge (SR-003) is
layered firmware-first (orderly shutdown at $\sim$3.0~V/cell via IF-008
telemetry -- close the SD log, send a final frame, halt) with a hardware
EN-pin UVLO backstop below that. One interaction between the two layers was
found and closed: the UVLO recovery point sits above the firmware shutdown
threshold, so at end of discharge the relaxing open-circuit cell voltage
could restart the converter and reboot the OBC into a loop. The fix is a
boot-time guard -- read the battery voltage first and stay halted if it is
still below the recovery point -- making the hardware backstop alone
sufficient to prevent over-discharge regardless of firmware state.

\textbf{Payload power control} uses the same GPIO-switched P-MOSFET as true
cutoff (the RT1176 cannot software-cut its own supply), paired with a UART
\texttt{SHUTDOWN} command so the payload quiesces before the rail is cut. A
bulk capacitor originally planned at the Coral connector was dropped after
bench measurement showed the real inference transient to be roughly five
times gentler than the worst-case assumption the budget had carried
(Section~\ref{sec:eps-verification}); the regulator's own capacitance
absorbs the load step without help.

The platform stacks as a 10$\times$10$\times$15~cm 1U volume: TT\&C and CDH
on top, batteries, EPS, OBC, and the Coral payload below. The EPS is a
hand-wired perfboard carrying the battery input, polyfuse, reverse-polarity
MOSFET, boost regulator, and telemetry divider, with header rows broken out
for the Coral power connector and the OBC power/telemetry lines.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:eps-verification}

The EPS was bench-tested 2026-07-07 against a bring-up ladder: visual
inspection, continuity, current-limited power-up, battery power-up,
increasingly realistic loads, ending with the Coral integration test. The
rail regulates at a constant 5.06~V across the full 3.0--4.2~V battery
range; the telemetry divider is exact; the SI2301 stage fully blocks a
reversed input; and the measured inference transient
($\sim$0.3~A above baseline, 2.0--2.4~ms, self-contained) is far gentler
than the multi-amp spike the original budget assumed.
