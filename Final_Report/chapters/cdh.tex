\chapter{Command and Data Handling (CDH)}

\section{Responsibilities}

CDH is the sensor-acquisition and data-management layer of the flight
software. In each major cycle it reads every sensor on the platform,
condenses the results into a single shared data structure -- the
\emph{datapool} -- and hands that snapshot to the rest of the flight
software (FSW, FDIR, TT\&C, and SD logging) to consume. Its
responsibilities are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sensor acquisition.} Drive the GPS (u-blox MAX-M10S), the
        IMU (MPU-6050), the barometer (MS5607), and the EPS battery-voltage
        monitor, each through a dedicated equipment handler.
  \item \textbf{Datapool ownership.} Own the datapool -- write it once per
        cycle so every consumer reads one internally consistent snapshot.
  \item \textbf{Data validation.} Range-, plausibility-, and timeout-check
        every sensor stream, and publish a per-sensor \texttt{*\_valid} flag
        so consumers never act on stale or impossible data
        (Section~\ref{sec:cdh-validation}).
  \item \textbf{Shared-bus ownership.} Own the I2C1 hardware that the
        environmental sensors share, and execute the multi-tick bus-restart
        sequence when FDIR requests recovery -- CDH is the sole writer of
        \texttt{i2c\_bus\_state}.
  \item \textbf{Sensor verification.} Confirm each sensor against
        controlled physical stimuli before flight
        (Section~\ref{sec:cdh-verification}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Interfaces}

The primary interface CDH exposes is not a bus but a data structure. CDH is
a producer: it turns raw peripheral traffic into the datapool, and every
other subsystem is a consumer of that datapool (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-flow}).
The GPS, IMU, and barometer share a single I2C bus that CDH owns; the
battery pack voltage is read on an ADC input from the EPS telemetry divider
(IF-008, described in the EPS chapter). The overall software structure --
equipment handlers feeding the datapool, and the datapool feeding every
other subsystem -- is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cdh-architecture}.

The entry points are deliberately narrow. \texttt{CDH\_Init()} brings up
every handler once before the superloop starts and records the ground
pressure baseline; \texttt{CDH\_Update(dp, scv)} runs the entire
acquire-validate-publish cycle for one tick; and
\texttt{CDH\_RequestBusRestart()} is the single call FDIR uses to ask CDH to
recover the I2C bus -- one instance of the general detect/request/execute/
acknowledge pattern FDIR uses for every equipment fault, described once in
the Flight Software chapter (Section~\ref{sec:fsw-fdir}).

\section{Design Decisions}

\subsection{The Datapool}
\label{sec:cdh-datapool}

All inter-subsystem state is held in a single shared structure, the
datapool, defined once in the interface-control header and populated by CDH.
Rather than each subsystem polling the sensors on an independent schedule,
CDH writes the datapool in its entirety once per cycle, and all other
subsystems read from it. It is organised by source, and each sensor group
carries its own validity flag (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-datapool}).

\midrule

IMU & \texttt{imu\_accel\_\{x,y,z\}\_g}, \texttt{imu\_accel\_mag\_g}, \texttt{imu\_gyro\_\{x,y,z\}\_dps} & \texttt{imu\_valid} \\
Barometer & \texttt{baro\_pressure\_pa}, \texttt{baro\_alt\_m}, \texttt{baro\_temp\_c} & \texttt{baro\_valid} \\

Three principles define the datapool contract. First, \textbf{a single
writer}: only \texttt{CDH\_Update} writes \texttt{g\_datapool}, which
obviates locking in the single-threaded superloop and guarantees a
consistent snapshot. Second, \textbf{explicit validity}: when a sensor
fails a check, its group's \texttt{*\_valid} flag is cleared while the last
values are retained, so a consumer that observes the flag degrades
gracefully, while one that does not still reads the last valid sample
rather than a spurious zero. Third, \textbf{raw units}: fields are stored
in their source units (degrees, g, Pa, mV), with engineering conversion
deferred to the ground station; this relieves the flight processor of
unnecessary floating-point formatting, consistent with the telemetry
packet.

\textbf{Memory footprint.} The datapool is small enough to be negligible
against the memory budget of the STM32L476RG (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-memory}).
The live datapool and its persistent shadow together occupy 131~B of the
128~KB SRAM -- under 0.1\% -- and even the downlink packet that
re-serialises them adds only $\sim$155~B. Storing raw fixed-width types
rather than expanded engineering structures preserves this margin; the
dominant SRAM demand on the platform is the payload image frame, processed
in 512-byte chunks and never buffered in full, rather than the housekeeping
state.

\subsection{Non-Volatile Datapool Backup}
\label{sec:cdh-nvm}

The SD card is the primary housekeeping record; the datapool black-box is a
redundant copy in the microcontroller's internal flash for when a card is
absent, corrupt, or unresponsive. Its write path is fully independent of the SD
subsystem -- CPU and on-chip flash only, no SPI -- so a usable record survives
an SD failure, and because the datapool is stored unfiltered each record also
captures the fault state itself (cleared validity flags, live
\texttt{timestamp\_ms}).

\textbf{Flash budget.} The store reserves 104~KB -- 52 of the 2~KB pages at the
top of flash, just below the 2~KB SCV page -- kept out of the firmware image by
reducing the linker's usable flash to 918~KB
(\texttt{STM32L476RGTX\_FLASH.ld}). With the linked image under 175~KB, over
740~KB stays free (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-nvm-budget}).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Region} & \textbf{Size} & \textbf{Share} \\
\midrule
Firmware image (\texttt{.text}/\texttt{.rodata}/\texttt{.data})   & $<$175~KB & $<$18\% \\
Unused / available                                                & $>$743~KB & $>$72\% \\
Datapool backup (\texttt{0x080E5800}--\texttt{0x080FF7FF})        & 104~KB       & 10.2\% \\
SCV persistence (\texttt{0x080FF800}--\texttt{0x080FFFFF})        & 2~KB         & 0.2\% \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total flash}                                              & \textbf{1024~KB} & \textbf{100\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{STM32L476RG flash budget, including the reserved non-volatile regions.}
\label{tab:cdh-nvm-budget}
\end{table}

\textbf{Storage scheme.} The region is a circular log of 104-byte records --
16-bit magic, 16-bit sequence number, the 98-byte datapool, and a CRC-16 -- 19
per 2~KB page. A record is appended every 30~s; a page is erased only when the
write cursor first enters it, so wear is one erase per page per lap, not per
record. Records are ordered by sequence number rather than
\texttt{timestamp\_ms}, which resets to zero on every boot whereas the recorder
must stay coherent across resets.

\textbf{Capacity and cut-off.} The 988 slots span 8.2~h at 30~s. An 8~h
run-time timer then freezes the log; the region is sized so those 960 records
fill the ring without wrapping, capturing the whole flight from lift-off with a
$\sim$14-minute margin before the ring could lap (with the timer disabled it
falls back to circular behaviour, keeping the most recent 8.2~h). Worst-case
loss on an unclean reset is one interval. Filling once per flight, each page is
erased about once per flight -- of order $10^{4}$ flights against the flash
endurance -- so endurance is not a constraint.

\subsection{Equipment Handler Pattern}
\label{sec:cdh-handlers}

Each sensor is wrapped in an \emph{equipment handler} that follows one
uniform value-semantics pattern:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=C]
Handler H_Init(bus);              /* construct + first read      */
H = H_Update(H, bus);             /* re-read, returns new state  */
\end{lstlisting}

The handler struct carries everything that sensor needs to manage itself:
its latest reading, its previous reading, a local health flag, and the
timestamps that drive its cadence. Passing the handler by value in and out
of \texttt{Update} makes each sensor's state self-contained and its update a
pure state transition, which keeps \texttt{CDH\_Update} a flat, readable
sequence of calls with no hidden global sensor state. Each handler also
owns its own \emph{rate}. The superloop calls \texttt{CDH\_Update} every
100~ms (10~Hz); within that cycle a handler samples at the major rate or
slower on its own timer, and a low-rate sensor such as the GPS is ingested
whenever a fresh message has arrived (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-handlers}).

\textbf{Timing budget.} The 100~ms cycle is a soft gate: an overrun merely
delays the next cycle rather than missing a hard deadline, the one hard
constraint being the independent watchdog (IWDG $<$10~s, FR-011). Within that
budget CDH's own worst-case load is modest (Table~\ref{tab:cdh-timing}). The
dominant blocking term is the barometer's two 10~ms OSR-4096 conversions
(20~ms, and only every 500~ms); a datapool page erase ($\sim$25~ms) lands only
on the rare cycle that crosses a flash page, roughly once every 9~minutes. The
busiest CDH cycle -- a barometer read coinciding with a page erase -- still
sums to under 50~ms.

\textbf{GPS (MAX-M10S).} The receiver is driven over I2C in u-blox's native
UBX binary protocol rather than NMEA text. At start-up the handler
configures the module through the M10 configuration interface
(UBX-CFG-VALSET, RAM layer): it enables UBX output on I2C, disables the
NMEA protocol, requests the \texttt{NAV-PVT} navigation solution at 1~Hz,
and selects the \emph{Airborne $<$1\,g} dynamic model so the navigation
filter does not clamp the rapid altitude changes of flight. Each 100-byte
\texttt{NAV-PVT} message delivers position, ellipsoidal and mean-sea-level
altitude, the three velocity components, ground speed, heading, fix type,
satellite count, and per-solution accuracy estimates in a single
checksum-protected frame -- replacing the multi-sentence NMEA parse with
one binary decode and giving the FSW a directly usable vertical velocity.

\textbf{IMU (MPU-6050).} Sampled every cycle (10~Hz), the fastest sensor in
the loop; the handler flags a fault on repeated I2C read failures or a
flatlined output, and a failed read is never allowed to pass off the
previous sample as fresh.

\textbf{Barometer (MS5607).} Sampled at 2~Hz. The driver returns an absolute
pressure altitude; CDH subtracts a ground baseline captured at boot, so
\texttt{baro\_alt\_m} is reported as height above the launch site.

\subsection{Sensor Validation and Fault Handling}
\label{sec:cdh-validation}

Validation is layered. Each handler catches faults local to its own device
-- a GPS with no fix, an IMU whose output has flatlined, a barometer that
fails to acknowledge on the bus. On top of that, a central validation pass
(\texttt{SensorValidation\_Update}) runs every cycle after all handlers
have reported and applies cross-checks that need the assembled datapool:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Barometer.} Reject non-physical pressure
        ($<0$ or $>1519$~hPa) or temperature ($<-100$\,\textdegree C).
  \item \textbf{GPS.} Require a non-zero fix; reject altitude outside
        $0$--$40$~km; reject a solution more than 1000~km from the launch
        site (a coarse geofence around Munich); and declare the receiver
        stale if no new fix arrives for 60~s.
  \item \textbf{IMU.} Declare the sensor failed if the acceleration
        magnitude stays below the flatline threshold for longer than 4~s.
\end{itemize}

A failed check clears the datapool validity flag for that group and
increments a persistent fault counter, but leaves the last values in place.
Recovery is graded by how many sensors are affected. A \emph{single} failed
sensor triggers a recovery attempt that is then retried no more than once
per 60~s, avoiding a tight retry loop that would starve the rest of the
cycle. \emph{Multiple} simultaneous sensor failures are read as a
shared-bus problem rather than independent device faults, so validation
calls \texttt{CDH\_RequestBusRestart()}; because CDH owns the I2C hardware,
it -- not the validation code -- carries out the restart across subsequent
ticks and republishes \texttt{i2c\_bus\_state}. The whole scheme is
fail-soft: a degraded sensor removes its own data from the solution without
ever stalling the major cycle or the subsystems downstream of it.
